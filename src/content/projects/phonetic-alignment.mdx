## Overview

**Phonetic Alignment** is a Thai forced-alignment pipeline built on top of MFA (Montreal Forced Aligner). The standard MFA pipeline for Thai relies on Phonetisaurus for grapheme-to-phoneme conversion and was trained on limited Thai data — producing alignment boundaries only accurate to ~100ms. This project replaces both the phonemizer and the acoustic model to achieve sub-millisecond precision.

---

## What was improved

### Phonemizer: Phonetisaurus → Transformers

Phonetisaurus uses a weighted finite-state transducer approach to G2P conversion. For Thai — a tonal, scriptio continua language with complex syllable structure — this produces frequent misreadings of edge cases.

We replaced it with a fine-tuned Transformer-based G2P model trained on curated Thai lexicon data, yielding more accurate phoneme sequences as input to the aligner.

### Acoustic model: fine-tuned on refined Thai data

The MFA acoustic model was retrained from scratch on a curated Thai speech corpus with cleaned transcriptions and verified alignments. This substantially improves the model's ability to locate phoneme boundaries precisely within the audio signal.

---

## Results

Boundary precision improved from the MFA baseline of ~100ms to ~1ms — a 100× gain — enabling reliable phoneme-level supervision for downstream TTS and ASR training.

---

## Applications

- Phoneme-level forced alignment for Thai TTS training data
- Boundary supervision for Thai ASR fine-tuning
- Phonetic analysis and linguistic research on Thai speech
